Your pace normalised to flat-equivalent using the Minetti 2002 cost-of-locomotion polynomial. A 7:12 GAP on this climb-heavy loop means you'd run ~7:12/mi on a flat course at the same metabolic cost.

Why shrinkage?

With only 6 upper-body sessions, your estimate is pulled toward the fleet prior (0.2) because 6 points is noisy. At n=40+, your data dominates. Efron-Morris 1977; Gelman BDA3.

What updates nightly

Your soreness prompts + next-morning HRV/RHR deltas feed the learner. Each new observation shrinks the CI and can shift the mean.

Design notes
  • CI band + prior tick + posterior dot — reads as a forest-plot, which physiologists will recognise on sight.
  • Every row shows its evidence tier; T4 is deliberately muted so users know to discount low-sample estimates.
  • No raw Bayesian jargon in the main UI — the "Why shrinkage?" footer is for the curious; most users can ignore it.

  • Colour-blind safe — every chip shows both tier letter and colour; screen-readers get a full aria-label like "evidence tier 2, strong, based on Minetti 2002".

Methodology viewer — how we compute MIM for you

Lives under Settings → Methodology (extends existing `Methodology.tsx`). Linkable from the Evidence-Tier Chip expanded popover.

Sprint 7 Methodology.tsx +
MIM methodology
1. What is MIM? 2. Inputs 3. Priors (OpenSim + GRADE) 4. Shrinkage (Efron-Morris) 5. Your personalisation 6. Worked example 7. Known limits 8. References

1. What is MIM?

MIM (Musculoskeletal Impact Modifier) is a per-sport multiplier we apply when summing your daily training load. Banister TRIMP treats 200 units of cycling TRIMP the same as 200 units of strength-lower TRIMP — biomechanically, they're not. MIM makes them comparable.

adjusted_load[sport] = banister_trimp[sport] × MIM[sport, athlete]
T2 Combines Banister 1991 (TRIMP · T1) + BA's novel per-user personalisation
