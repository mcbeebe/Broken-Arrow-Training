01

Executive summary

iRunFar is not a plan generator — it is the trail/ultra world's deepest, field-tested craft library (fueling, vert & descent execution, heat/altitude, power-hiking, form, recovery formulas, mind, special populations), written by the same coaching tradition two of our own methods come from (Koop and the Roches' SWAP). We are the inverse: an adaptive plan + AI-coach engine with strong physiology — VDOT pacing, a distance-aware ramp, an honesty/feasibility advisory layer, and a research-cited terrain & descent engine — that they structurally cannot match.

The competitive frame is their craft vs. our engine. We don't need to out-write iRunFar; we need to absorb their craft into our engine as rules, content, and coach knowledge — and switch on the physiology we've already built but left dormant.

The decisive finding

Our biggest gap is leverage, not capability

Twice over, we have already built the exact physiology iRunFar's most-read content proves athletes care about — and left it disconnected from the plan an athlete actually trains by.

① Terrain & Descent physics BUILT · DORMANT

Minetti cost-of-gradient GAP, hiking locomotion, Vertical Efficiency (terrain/) and eccentric load + repeated-bout protection (Hyldahl 2017) + DOMS forecast (descent/) — research-cited, real, a PR-07→11 series. But no generated workout prescribes a climb or a downhill rep; descentCapacity is only a passive Dashboard metric. iRunFar dedicates three articles to quads/descending and calls "dead quads" the #1 DNF cause.

② Readiness / TRIMP / sleep BUILT · UNDER-USED

Our readiness engine already ingests HRV, resting HR, sleep quality & Body Battery and shows scores on the Dashboard. But it doesn't prescribe sleep, flag overtraining, or auto-adjust the plan — exactly the Recovery / Overtraining / Sleep ("the missing ingredient") triad iRunFar devotes whole sections to.

⇒ Highest-ROI work is wire what we built + add fueling — not invent new science.

04

What iRunFar teaches — the canon

Their "Guide to Everything" organizes the sport into 15 sections; the house consensus across Torrence, Uhan (PT), the Roches (SWAP), Koop (CTS), and Malcolm, with the prescriptions that recur.

Engine & structure

  • Aerobic "foundation-first," polarized — easy stays easy; gray-zone drift ("Aerobic Deficiency Syndrome") is the enemy. HR via MAF 180−age (max-aerobic; 200/210−age for AnT/VO2).
  • A 4-family workout taxonomy (Torrence): Endurance (<75% HRmax; carb-depleting ≤3 h; fast-finish final 2–10 mi @ MP), Stamina/LT (83–90%), Speed/VO2 (1–6 min @ 94–98%), Sprint/Hill — plus two-a-days (75+ mi/wk; 20–60 min), group/fartlek & out-and-back benchmark runs.
  • Periodization: base → introductory → specific → recovery; ≤10–15%/wk, recovery wk −50%; "spicy work — back-to-backs, climbs, quad-seasoning descents — late." Peak window ~2 wk (last long run 90 min easy 1 wk out); off-season 1–2 mo ("rest until hungry, +4–7 d").
  • Volume by distance: base ~50 mi / 8 h-wk → ceiling 60–70 mi / 8–10 h; long run = 50K 3–4 h · 100M 5–6 h · 200+ 5–10+ h; back-to-backs cut 25–50% off day-1; run by time-on-feet.
  • Race-specific tune-ups on a schedule (100M: 50K ~12 wk out, 50M/100K ~6 wk) + a dress-rehearsal predictor 4–6 wk out at ~30–60% of course.

Trail / mountain craft

  • Vert tiers drive the plan: Flat <120 ft/mi · Mountainous 120–240 · "Colossal" >240.
  • Downhill is first-class & perishable — reps 1–2 min on 6–10%, long descents, "at least every 10–14 days"; muscle memory fades in ~2–3 wks; "dead quads" = #1 DNF.
  • Power-hiking is a trained skill & a winning tactic (46% of a UTMB podium) — build 20–30 → 90 min on >15–20% grade; "hike the ups" to keep big-vert days aerobic.
  • Effort over pace, always — HR / RPE / breath govern, not GPS.

Fueling, hydration & environment their science pillar

  • Carbs 150–300 cal/hr; multiple-transportable (glucose:fructose ~2:1) lifts the ceiling 60 → 75–90 g/hr; start ≤45 min; train the gut 4–6 wk out (SGLT1 upregulates in ~3 days). Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg (→2.5 peak; female/peri upper).
  • Hydration (contrarian): "drink to thirst," 400–800 ml/hr; sodium prevents neither hyponatremia nor cramps — overdrinking is the danger.
  • Caffeine 3–6 mg/kg, 60 min pre, re-dose 2 h+; habituation is a myth.
  • Heat: 7–10 days, 50–100 min/day in heat (or post-run sauna 20–30 min), start ~2 wk out, maintain every 3rd day. Altitude: 21–28 days, live-high/train-low, ferritin <35 gates response.

Durability, recovery & mind

  • Strength = heavy & periodized (70%+ 1RM, 2×/wk, 6–9 wk) → power in-season → drop race week.
  • Form (Uhan): cadence ~180, hip-hinge, "braking vs. center-of-mass, not foot-strike," a morning mobility flow.
  • Recovery formulas: 1 rest day / 10 mi; +1 h sleep / 10 mi; reverse-taper rebuild; Stoplight return-to-run by 24-h symptom trajectory; "injury vs. dysfunction."
  • Sleep = "the missing ingredient" — 7–9 h; +1 h measurably improves performance; extend gradually.
  • Overtraining: sleep disturbance + HR-drift-at-same-pace are leading indicators; cut volume 75–100%; periodize life stress.
  • Mind, rehearsed in training: mantras (≤8 words), aid-station segmenting, multisensory imagery, process > outcome goals.

Sources: the "Ultramarathon Training: A Guide to Everything" hub + its ~50 linked articles, plus the Fueling/Hydration, Heat, Strength, Form, Recovery, Mental, and Aging/Women's sub-hubs. Full list in §09.

06

Gaps & weaknesses (honest)

  • Built-but-dormant IP. Our most differentiated work (terrain/descent physics; readiness/sleep) is largely invisible in the plan the athlete trains by.
  • No fuel/hydration layer at all — the single most-asked endurance question, and iRunFar's deepest content, is missing.
  • No environmental prep — heat is absent; altitude is a stub.
  • The "execution" half of the sport is missing — pacing discipline, aid-station planning, power-hiking, the mental curriculum iRunFar argues is the ultra differentiator.
  • Durability is thin — no periodized runner strength, no form/cadence, no post-race recovery/sleep or return-to-run.
  • Metric framing — iRunFar leads with effort/time-on-feet for trail; we lead with pace/mileage despite owning the pieces.

Ranked roadmap — 14 updates

Ranked by impact × leverage. Each carries the CLAUDE.md Witchel 3-rule check (massive market · visceral solve · customer language); "wire existing" items rank up because the science is already built and tested.

Tier 1 · do firsthighest impact, strongest leverage
R1

Wire the Terrain + Descent engine into plan prescription

Effort: MediumWire existing

Generate weekly vert targets and scheduled downhill-repeat / mountain-climb sessions from the race elevation profile, driven by the descent engine's repeated-bout / DOMS forecast (re-stimulate every 10–14 days; spicy work late). Turn descentCapacity from a passive metric into a programmed progression. Bake in iRunFar's quad protocols — downhill-after-uphill repeats, aid-station transition runs, form-checks at cadence ~180 — to bulletproof the legs ("dead quads").

Witchel · market: trail/ultra is our core · solve: "my plan never trained climbing or descending and my quads blew up" · language: "vert," "climbing legs," "blown quads," "downhill reps."
R2

Add a Fueling & Hydration engine

Effort: MediumBiggest market gap

Race duration/intensity → carb g/hr (ramp 60→90, 2:1 glucose:fructose, start ≤45 min, 150–300 cal/hr) + a 4–6-wk gut-training progression + a tagged long-run fueling rehearsal; protein targets; hydration "drink to thirst" 400–800 ml/hr + hyponatremia caution; caffeine 3–6 mg/kg. Surface in plan + coach.

Witchel · market: every endurance athlete · solve: "I bonked / my gut shut down" · language: "bonk," "carbs per hour," "drink to thirst," "gut training."
R3

Heat-acclimation protocol module

Effort: Low–MedPairs w/ taper engine

Hot goal race → insert a 7–10 day protocol (50–100 min/day easy in heat, or post-run sauna 20–30 min) starting ~2 wk out, maintain every 3rd day, with decay rules; advisory + coach narration + calendar blocks.

Witchel · market: every hot goal race · solve: "I cooked and DNF'd in the heat" · language: "heat training," "sauna protocol," "acclimatize."
Tier 2 · high valuecraft layers — content/coach + light engine
R4

Structured trail-workout taxonomy

Medium

Replace generic "quality" with iRunFar-style sessions keyed to phase + vert tier — hill/downhill reps, gear-changing, strides, and a 4–6-wk-out dress-rehearsal predictor. (Overlaps R1 on hills/descents.)

R5

Recovery, sleep & overtraining protection

Low–MedWire readiness

Post-race rest-day (1/10 mi) + sleep (+1 h/10 mi; 7–9 h baseline, circadian/environment hygiene) formulas, a week-by-week return-to-normal (days 1–7 walk → wk 2–3 ≤1 h jogs → rebuild), reverse-taper, Stoplight return-to-run; and wire the readiness/HRV engine into overtraining flags — Torrence's Green/Yellow/Red color-code, a 1–3 hard-races/yr cap, morning-HR >5 bpm — + light plan auto-adjustment (our second dormant asset).

R6

Pacing & race-execution plan

Medium

A Rule-of-Thirds effort-cap pacing card (HR/RPE cap first third → even → finish), power-hike grade thresholds (>15–20%), and an aid-station / fuel-per-segment card generated from the course.

R7

Power-hiking as a prescribed skill

Low (after R1)Wire existing

Schedule power-hike sessions (20–30 → 90 min, >15–20% grade) and "hike-up/run-down" to keep big-vert days aerobic — uses the hiking-locomotion engine already built.

R8

Runner strength periodization

Medium

Off-season heavy maximal block (70%+ 1RM, 2×/wk, 6–9 wk) → in-season power → drop race week — plus the Trail-Worthy-Body bodyweight / plyo / balance circuits (core 5-min ×3/wk, plyometric, multi-planar, balance) as the durability layer; with the masters/menopause variants we partly have.

R9

Form & cadence coaching layer

Low

Cadence target (~170–180, + on descents), hip-hinge / braking-vs-COM / stride cues in the coach, optional cadence target on workouts, a morning mobility flow.

Tier 3 · differentiationdepth & special populations
R10

Mental-training curriculum

Mantras (≤8 words), aid-station segmenting, imagery + breath cue, process > outcome goals, plus iRunFar's frameworks — Necessary/Possible/Impossible, mindfulness M-fit attention training, and running-streak / gamification for consistency — rehearsed in training, narrated by the coach.

R11

Women's menstrual-cycle awareness

Follicular/luteal training-fuel-recovery nuance, RED-S screen (amenorrhea ≥3 mo), evidence-humble + self-tracking; reuse our menopause-research house style.

R12

Masters load logic

Beyond Tanaka maxHR: optional 1 hard session/week, longer recovery, protein emphasis, strength priority for 40+.

R13

Build out the altitude module

From the stub: per-elevation arrival windows (6,000 ft AMS ~6 h; 7,000 ft +20–30 s/mi), pre-altitude VO2 work 2–3 wk before, ferritin gate, live-high/train-low, VO2 −8–11%/1,000 m; advisory + coach.

R14

Trail-first framing

Offer time-on-feet as the primary long-run/volume metric (we own the time cap + GAP) and make periodization intent visible.

08

What to protect — our moat

iRunFar cannot generate or adapt a plan, can't personalize to your data, contradicts itself across authors, and has no feasibility/honesty layer. Keep leaning into adaptive multi-method generation, the honesty advisories (feasibility/runway/goal/experience), VDOT precision, readiness/TRIMP + (once wired) terrain/descent analytics, and multi-modal coverage (Hyrox / General Fitness). The strategy is to absorb iRunFar's field-tested craft into our engine — pairing their wisdom with our automation and our physics.
