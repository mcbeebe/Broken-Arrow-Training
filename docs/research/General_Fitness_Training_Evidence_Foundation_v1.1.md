## 1. Executive summary

The strongest, most convergent evidence supports a **blended, four-pillar program** rather than any single modality. Combining resistance training with aerobic activity is associated with **~40% lower all-cause mortality** versus doing neither — roughly triple the benefit of strength training alone (~15%). That synergy is the empirical case for the whole model.

The four pillars and the rules that bind them:

- **Aerobic base (Zone 2)** — the bulk of the weekly aerobic dose; builds mitochondria and VO₂max; well-tolerated at every level.
- **VO₂max / intervals** — the longevity multiplier: cardiorespiratory fitness is among the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality (elite vs. low fitness ~80% lower).
- **Strength** — ≥2 days/week is the health floor; the longevity-protective dose is modest, but the dial turns up sharply when the goal is muscle.
- **Mobility / stability** — a dynamic warm-up before every session, flexibility after, and balance training as a core component for older adults.

**Binding rules:** blend freely (the interference effect is negligible for non-elites) · ~80/20 easy:hard, model-agnostic · periodize loosely (any model) and drive progressive overload · deload on fatigue triggers, not a fixed peak.

## 2. Foundational weekly doses (Run 1)

Issued identically by the WHO (2020) and the US Physical Activity Guidelines (2018) — strong recommendations, moderate-certainty evidence. These are the floor the engine must always meet.

| Pillar | Minimum-effective | Optimal / note | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aerobic | 150 min/wk moderate **or** 75 min/wk vigorous | 150–300 min; benefit keeps rising past 300; 60+ benefit beyond guidelines (~35% lower mortality) | High |
| Strength | 2+ days/wk, all major muscle groups | No added population-mortality benefit from higher volume (distinct from hypertrophy — see §6) | High |
| Combined | Both of the above | **~40% lower all-cause mortality vs. neither (RR 0.60)** — the core case for blending | High |

- Aerobic dose-response is continuous: RR 0.86 at 150 min/wk → 0.74 at 300 min/wk (Samitz 2011, 1.3M participants).
- Any resistance training is associated with ~10–17% lower all-cause mortality vs. none (Momma 2022; Shailendra 2022).
- Resistance mortality benefit is **J-shaped** — peaks ~27% at only ~60 min/wk, then diminishes (Shailendra 2022). The longevity dose is LOW.
- Return-to-training / sarcopenia start: ~120 min/wk of moderate resistance over 3 sessions, progressing as tolerated (Yan 2025).

## 3. Pillar — Aerobic base (Zone 2) (Run 2)

Moderate-intensity continuous (Zone 2) training reliably raises mitochondrial volume density (SMD 1.04) and VO₂max (SMD 0.75; +9–19% over 6–16 wk) and is well-tolerated — a valid primary aerobic base at all levels (2026 MICT meta-analysis).

**Caveat:** the "Zone 2 is *optimal*" claim is NOT proven — that narrative came from elite observational data (2025 *Sports Medicine* review, Gibala/Gurd). Treat Zone 2 as a strong base, not a silver bullet; pair it with intervals.

| Zone | % HRmax | % HRR | Field cue (no equipment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 2 / easy | 64–76% | 40–59% | Talk Test: full comfortable sentences, but cannot sing. Last "comfortable-speech" stage ≈ first ventilatory threshold (VT1). |
| VO₂max intervals | 90–95% | — | Hard, talking limited to a few words (see §4). |

**Design note:** fixed %HRmax maps to inconsistent physiological states across people (CV 6–29%). Present %HRmax as a *starting estimate* and offer the Talk Test / VT1 as the individualizing anchor.

---

## 4. Pillar — VO₂max / intervals (Run 2)

**Why it matters most for longevity:** in 122,007 patients, elite vs. low cardiorespiratory fitness carried an adjusted HR of 0.20 — ~80% lower all-cause mortality — with low fitness a worse risk factor than coronary artery disease (1.29) or smoking (1.41) (Mandsager 2018, *JAMA Network Open*).

| Protocol | Prescription | Effect (8 wk) |
|---|---|---|
| Norwegian 4×4 | 4 min @ 90–95% HRmax / 3 min active @ 70%, ×4; ~1–3×/wk | +7.2% VO₂max |
| Short intervals 15/15 | 15 s @ 90–95% HRmax / 15 s @ 70%, repeated; ~3×/wk | +5.5% VO₂max |

Helgerud 2007 (RCT). Both are effective, time-efficient options — but note: when total work is matched, HIIT was **not** proven superior to steady-state for VO₂max (that claim was refuted). Prescribe intervals as effective, not as a replacement for the Zone 2 base.

## 5. Aerobic intensity distribution (Run 2)

~80% easy / ~20% hard is a defensible default — but for non-elite trainees the distribution **MODEL** (polarized vs. pyramidal vs. threshold) is not load-bearing: a 2025 IPD network meta-analysis co-authored by Seiler (the polarized model's originator) found POL vs. PYR null overall, with a significant performance-level interaction — recreational athletes did at least as well pyramidal.

- Do not force a strict polarized split on general-fitness users; keep it simple (~80/20) and flexible.
- Beginners / deconditioned users cannot reliably sustain a high hard-fraction — they drift pyramidal on their own. Weight beginners toward low-intensity base work and add intensity conservatively.

## 6. Pillar — Strength (Runs 1 & 3)

**The key divergence the engine must respect:** the longevity-protective strength dose is LOW (J-shaped, peak ~30–60 min/wk), while the dose for muscle SIZE is GRADED and higher. These are different goals on the same pillar — the basis for Stay Healthy vs. Build Muscle being distinct presets.

| Goal | Weekly volume | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Health / longevity | Meet the 2 days/wk floor; ~30–60 min/wk total is the mortality sweet spot | Shailendra 2022; WHO (no added mortality benefit above floor) |
| Muscle (hypertrophy) | ~9 sets/muscle/wk = significant growth; 18–27 sets for more; graded ~+0.37%/set | Schoenfeld 2017; Heaselgrave 2019 |

Intensity: a broad load range works; reps-in-reserve (RIR) / RPE is a useful autoregulation tool for experienced lifters but is less accurate in novices — beginners do better with simpler structured loading.

## 7. Concurrent (hybrid) training — the interference effect (Run 3)

**Verdict for general fitness: largely a non-issue.** A 43-study meta-analysis (Schumann 2022) found concurrent training does NOT compromise hypertrophy (SMD −0.01) or maximal strength (SMD −0.06); only explosive power is modestly attenuated (SMD −0.28), mostly in same-session work. Interference is training-status-dependent — it emerges in already-trained, high-volume athletes, not beginners (Coffey & Hawley).

| Sequencing rule | Basis |
|---|---|
| Beginners/intermediates: combine cardio + lifting freely; no mitigation needed unless explosive power is the goal. | Schumann 2022; Coffey & Hawley 2016 |
| If a single shared session is unavoidable: **lift BEFORE cardio** (protects lower-body dynamic strength, +6.91%). Otherwise order is optional. | Eddens 2017 (order affects only this one outcome) |
| Choose endurance modality (run vs. bike) and weekly cardio volume for the user's goals & joints — **NOT** as interference levers. | Modality & dose claims were refuted (§12) |

## 8. Periodization, progression & deload (Run 3)

- **Periodize, but the model is interchangeable.** Structured > unstructured for strength (ES 0.43, larger in novices; Williams 2017), yet linear = daily-undulating for hypertrophy (Grgic 2017). Pick any model by adherence/preference; drive progress through volume + progressive overload.
- **Autoregulation (RIR/RPE-based load) is equivalent — not superior** — to percentage-based loading, and the evidence is trained-only. Reserve it for experienced users; for beginners prefer simple structured loading.
- **Deload on fatigue TRIGGERS, not a peaking schedule.** No single overtraining biomarker is valid (ECSS/ACSM consensus) — monitor a panel: session performance, RPE trends, sleep, mood/wellness. Functional overreaching is reversible with adequate recovery.
- **Open item:** no validated fixed deload cadence exists for non-peaking fitness — use trigger-based deloads with a sensible default (e.g., a lighter week every ~4–8 weeks). This is a design decision, not an evidence parameter.

## 9. Pillar — Mobility / flexibility / stability (Run 3)

- **Dynamic warm-up before EVERY session:** submaximal aerobic → large-amplitude dynamic stretching → activity-specific drills (the RAMP pattern); ~7–10 min (Behm & Chaouachi 2011).
- **Static stretching:** ≥60 s/muscle acutely cuts strength ~5.4% (Simic 2013) — place substantive flexibility work AFTER training. Brief (<60 s) pre-session stretching is harmless if followed by dynamic movement.
- **Balance / stability is the single strongest fall-prevention modality for older adults:** −24% fall rate (RaR 0.76, high-certainty; Sherrington/Cochrane 2020). Prescribe for 65+ as a core component; combine with strength; ≥8 wk, sessions ≥30 min.

## 10. Integrated weekly model & parameter ranges

The balanced ("Stay Healthy") default, by training availability. Day-count templates are design inference — the evidence supports the principles (order, separation, structure, ~80/20), not numbers keyed to a specific weekly frequency.

| Days/wk | Zone 2 cardio | VO₂max intervals | Strength | Mobility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1 session | within a cardio day | 2 (full-body) | warm-up each session; stretch after |
| 4 | 2 sessions | 1 session | 2 (full-body / U-L) | + 1 short mobility block |
| 5 | 2–3 sessions | 1 session | 2–3 | warm-up each; balance for 65+ |
| 6 | 3 sessions | 1–2 sessions | 3 (U-L or PPL) | daily warm-up; standalone flexibility |

**Presets re-weight the same four pillars** (the engine is one model; goals are dials):

| Pillar / dial | Stay Healthy | Build Muscle | Build Endurance | Lose Fat \* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 2 volume | bulk of 150–300 min | maintain ~150 min | push toward 300+ min | high volume |
| VO₂max intervals | 1×/wk | 0–1×/wk | 1–2×/wk | 1–2×/wk |
| Strength sets/muscle/wk | ~6–10 (≥2 days) | 12–20+ (graded) | maintain ~6 (≥2 days) | ~10–15 (spare lean mass) |
| Mobility/stability | standard | standard | standard | standard |

\* Lose Fat dosing was provisional in v1.0; now evidence-graded in Appendix A.

## 11. Personalization levers

- **Experience:** beginners → more Zone 2, conservative intensity, simple structured loading; periodization benefit is actually larger in novices. Advanced → autoregulation available, higher strength volume, watch interference only at high cardio loads.
- **Age (60+):** prescribing ABOVE the aerobic floor is beneficial where tolerated; balance/stability becomes a core pillar, not optional.
- **Injury / return-to-training:** start low (e.g., sarcopenia model ~120 min/wk strength over 3 sessions) and progress as tolerated; lean on low-impact cross-training modalities.

## 12. Do NOT build these in (refuted in verification)

These plausible-sounding claims were tested and failed adversarial verification. They must not become engine rules:

- Vigorous intensity has a distinct, larger per-hour mortality benefit than moderate (Run 1).
- Hypertrophy follows an inverted-U with a peak at ~18 sets/wk (Run 1).
- High intensity beats Zone 2 / MICT for the general public; HIIT beats steady-state when work-matched (Run 2).
- A strict polarized model is clearly superior for non-elite trainees (Run 2).
- Endurance modality (running vs. cycling) drives interference; interference scales with cardio dose; a hard ≥3 h intra-day spacing rule (Run 3).
- Daily-undulating periodization is superior for maximal strength; autoregulation is superior (not just equivalent) to %-based loading (Run 3).
- A specific "3+ hr/wk including balance → −42% falls" dosing target (Run 3).

---

## 13. Open design decisions (evidence-silent)

The evidence deliberately does not settle these — they are explicit engineering choices, not guesses:

- Fixed deload cadence (use trigger-based + a sensible ~4–8 wk default).
- Exact 3/4/5/6-day templates (inferred from the principles above).
- Optimal mobility/balance minutes/week for younger general-fitness adults (strong dosing evidence exists only for older-adult balance).
- Intra-day cardio/strength spacing for non-power goals (the ≥3 h rule was underpowered).

## Appendix A — Goal-preset evidence layer (v1.1)

**Verification status.** The General Fitness path offers four goal presets over the same four-pillar engine. **Lose Fat is adversarially verified** (3-vote, 21 confirmed claims). **Build Muscle and Build Endurance are extracted from primary meta-analyses but PENDING verification** — a degraded API blocked their verification pass; the conclusions below are mainstream and low-controversy, to be upgraded to fully-verified later.

### Lose Fat — ✓ verified

- Training's job is to PRESERVE lean mass + metabolic rate; **diet drives the deficit.** Aerobic exercise alone is a modest fat-loss lever (~0.52 kg per 30 min/wk; ~1.6 kg over 6 mo). *[High — Thorogood 2011; Jayedi 2024]*
- **Strength is the anchor:** it preserves fat-free mass in a deficit (untrained/obese can even gain). A MODERATE volume (~3 sets/exercise, ~10–12 sets/muscle/wk) preserves muscle as well as higher volume — don't escalate, don't cut. Dieting still blunts NEW muscle (ES −0.58): target maintenance/recomposition and train heavy to keep strength. *[High — ISSN 2017; Murphy & Koehler 2022; Roth 2023]*
- **Cardio: HIIT ≈ steady-state** for body composition — choose by time-efficiency (HIIT ~40% less time) and recoverability, not a "fat-burning zone." Cardio cuts visceral/waist fat at HIGH GRADE certainty even when scale weight barely moves. *[High — Wewege 2017; Jayedi 2024]*
- **Density / circuit (metabolic RT)** is a viable two-birds option (fat −4.3%, muscle +1.9%) when sets are near failure — time-efficient, but not a license to merely shorten rest at light loads. *[verified, caveated — Ramos-Campo 2021]*
- **Rate: 0.5–1.0% bodyweight/week** (slower preserves lean mass). Daily-step floor ~8,000–8,500/day as a NEAT/expenditure and anti-regain lever. *[High — Helms/Garthe via ISSN 2017; El Ghoch 2026]*
- **Preset deltas vs. balanced:** strength held at a muscle-sparing dose (~10–12 sets/muscle/wk, heavy); cardio UP (dosed for sustainability + visceral fat; HIIT for time-efficiency); optional density/circuit block; prescribe a daily-step floor.

### Build Muscle — ⚠ extracted (pending verification)

- Hypertrophy occurs across a wide load range (~30%+ of 1RM) IF sets are taken near failure — there is no single "hypertrophy rep zone."
- Training to momentary failure is NOT required; proximity-to-failure (~0–3 RIR) plus weekly volume (~12–20 sets/muscle) are the drivers.
- Frequency: training a muscle 2×/week beats 1× (volume equated); 3× is not clearly better than 2×.
- Heavy loads still matter specifically for MAXIMAL strength (load is dominant for 1RM); proximity-to-failure drives hypertrophy, not strength.
- **Preset deltas vs. balanced:** strength-volume dial UP (~12–20 sets/muscle/wk, 2×/wk per muscle, ~0–3 RIR; loads flexible ~6–15 reps but include heavy work); cardio kept at the health-maintaining floor (interference is negligible at this level — see §7).

### Build Endurance — ⚠ extracted (pending verification)

- Threshold / tempo work is a ~1×/week ACCENT, not the backbone — polarized ≥ threshold-centric distributions, and threshold-only fails to improve VO₂ in the already-fit. Keep easy / long-slow-distance dominant.
- Heavy strength (≥80% 1RM, near-maximal) improves running and cycling ECONOMY at a low dose (1–2×/week); heavy load beats plyometrics for economy.
- A tempo session ≈ 20–40 min continuous "comfortably hard" (slightly above lactate threshold).
- **Preset deltas vs. balanced:** aerobic volume UP toward 300+ min/wk (progress gradually, ~10%/wk guideline); keep ~80/20; add ONE weekly threshold/tempo accent and retain VO₂max intervals; include a weekly long easy session; strength held NEAR the floor but kept HEAVY (1–2×/wk) for economy.

### Preset dials — summary

| Pillar / dial | Stay Healthy | Lose Fat ✓ | Build Muscle ⚠ | Build Endur. ⚠ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 2 cardio | 150–300 min | ↑ + density | ~150 min (floor) | ↑↑ 300+ min |
| VO₂max / HIIT | 1×/wk | 1–2×/wk (time-eff.) | 0–1×/wk | 1–2×/wk + tempo |
